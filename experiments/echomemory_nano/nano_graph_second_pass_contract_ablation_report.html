EchoMemory Graph Second-Pass Contract Ablation

This is a deliberately narrow experiment. It does not claim that one extra graph pass solves all QA problems. It tests one sharper question: when the planner says a temporal query should have both chronology evidence and event support, does a self-check-driven graph second-pass actually complete that evidence contract?

Cases6
One-pass Contract OK0
Graph Second-pass Contract OK4
Improved Cases4

Interpretation

What is being measured

We judge each run by two criteria: keyword correctness and layer-contract completeness. For example, temporal queries in this nano planner want both temporal_tree and event.

Why this is relevant

This mirrors the current main-code direction: self-check first diagnoses evidence weakness, then a conservative graph second-pass tries to补足 missing support.

What this does not claim

It is not a general benchmark. It is a method ablation about evidence-shape repair, closer to a paper figure than to a product QA leaderboard.

Summary

Keyword coverage stayed at 6 / 6 for one-pass and 6 / 6 after graph second-pass. The bigger change is contract completeness: 0 / 6 -> 4 / 6.

Improved cases: temporal_leave_date, temporal_join_date, temporal_helper_date, temporal_lease_date
Triggered second-pass cases: temporal_leave_date, temporal_join_date, temporal_helper_date, temporal_lease_date

Why this matters for the paper line

Self-RAG flavor

The point is not just “retrieve once and answer”. It is closer to the Self-RAG idea that evidence quality should decide whether the system keeps searching.

MemoRAG / coarse-to-fine

One-pass tree retrieval is the coarse stage. Graph second-pass is the fine support stage that only runs when evidence shape is incomplete.

LongMemEval-style auditability

This experiment explicitly measures evidence completeness, not just whether a surface keyword shows up. That is closer to memory-system evaluation than a raw QA scoreboard.
